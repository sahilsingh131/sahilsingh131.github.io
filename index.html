Challenge 1 Homepage

The blue heart of our planet

Every second breath you take comes from the ocean. Tiny drifting plants called phytoplankton make a huge share of Earth’s oxygen, and the sea absorbs about 30% of the CO₂ we release and over 90% of the extra heat trapped by greenhouse gases.

In 2015, all 193 UN member states agreed to 17 Sustainable Development Goals to reach by 2030. Goal 14, Life Below Water, commits the world to conserve and sustainably use the oceans, seas and marine resources.

But the ocean is under more pressure than ever. Overfishing, plastic, warming water and acidification are pushing marine life to the edge.

Summary

What the data tells us

🎣

Fishing is getting worse overall

Sustainable fish stocks fell from 90% in 1974 to 62.4%. But 72.6% of the fish actually landed now come from sustainable stocks, so good management works where it’s used.

🪸

Reefs are in crisis

NOAA satellite data recorded bleaching-level heat across 84% of reef areas in at least 82 countries between 2023 and 2025.

🛟

Protection is far behind

Only 8.4% of the ocean is protected, and much less is fully protected, well short of the 30% goal.

🌡️

The water is changing

Monitoring stations show seawater CO₂ rising and pH falling, while sea level and ocean heat keep climbing.

🧴

Plastic is everywhere

Millions of tonnes of plastic reach waterways every year, much of it carried by rivers in crowded coastal regions.

🌏

Real progress, too

The High Seas Treaty came into force in January 2026, allowing protected areas in international waters for the first time.
