What is Goal 14?

In 2015, every UN member country signed up to 17 goals to make the world fairer and more sustainable by 2030. Goal 14 is the one about the sea.

Its official aim is to “conserve and sustainably use the oceans, seas and marine resources.” In plain terms: stop taking more from the ocean than it can replace, and stop putting things into it that harm it.

That matters to everyone, not just people who live near the coast. The ocean absorbs about 30% of the carbon dioxide we release and more than 90% of the extra heat trapped by greenhouse gases. Over 3 billion people depend on marine and coastal life for their jobs and food.

Right now, things are heading the wrong way. More fish stocks are overfished than ever, most of the world’s coral reefs have just been through the worst bleaching on record, and only a small part of the ocean is protected.

Overfishing

Boats catch fish faster than they can breed. More than a third of fish stocks are now overfished.

Pollution

Plastic, sewage and farm fertiliser wash into the sea and create dead zones where almost nothing lives.

Warming

Marine heatwaves bleach coral and push fish toward the poles, away from the people who rely on them.

Acidification

CO₂ dissolving in seawater makes it more acidic, so shellfish and coral struggle to build their shells and skeletons.

Why coral turns white

Corals get their colour, and most of their food, from tiny algae that live inside them. When the water gets too hot for too long, the coral pushes the algae out and turns white. If the heat doesn’t ease off, the coral starves and dies.

Reefs cover less than 1% of the sea floor but are home to around a quarter of all marine species.

The 2023–25 event hit reefs in at least 82 countries.

The ten targets

Goal 14 is split into ten targets. The first seven are things to achieve. The last three (14.a to 14.c) are about how countries will get there.

What the data shows

Fishing is getting worse overall. The share of fish stocks fished sustainably has dropped from 90% in 1974 to 62.4%. But 72.6% of the fish actually caught now come from sustainable stocks, so the places that manage fishing well are working.

Reefs are in serious trouble. Satellite temperature data from NOAA Coral Reef Watch showed bleaching-level heat over 84% of reef areas between 2023 and 2025.

Protection is way behind. Only 8.4% of the ocean is protected, and a lot less than that is fully protected from fishing and mining.

The water is changing. Long-term measuring stations show seawater CO₂ rising and pH falling, while sea level and ocean heat keep going up.

There is some good news. The High Seas Treaty came into force in January 2026. For the first time, countries can create protected areas in international waters.

Challenge 5: if we had $5 million

Our $5 million plan for Indonesia

If we had US$5 million to spend on Goal 14, we would spend it in Indonesia. It sits in the middle of the Coral Triangle, the richest area of sea life on Earth, and millions of people there depend on reefs and fishing.

Indonesia also has some of the problems Goal 14 is trying to solve: plastic pouring out of rivers, damaged reefs and overfishing. Local reef restoration projects in Sulawesi have already shown what works, so the money could build on them.

  • More than 17,000 islands
  • One of the longest coastlines in the world
  • One of the biggest fishing nations
  • A major source of river plastic reaching the sea

Where the money goes

Team up with local groups and village councils. Install river barriers, hire rangers, train reef restorers and survey the starting condition of each site.
